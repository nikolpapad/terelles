# Marseille RPG - Tone & visual style
 
## The feeling
 
A sunny afternoon in Marseille seen through a cozy pixel-art adventure. The player should feel curious, welcome and capable, like exploring their own city with fresh eyes.
 
The tone is **warm, hopeful and playful**. The game talks about real environmental problems (floods, fires, waste, pollution), but it never looks sad, dark or scary. Every problem is shown as a mission to solve, not a disaster. Think "let's fix this together", not "the planet is dying".
 
Mood references: cozy pixel RPGs in the spirit of Stardew Valley or Eastward for the warmth and detail, with the bright clarity of a summer postcard from the Mediterranean.
 
## Marseille, recognisable at a glance
 
The city is the star. A kid from Marseille should instantly spot:
 
- the **turquoise sea** and white limestone of the Calanques,
- the **Bonne Mère** watching over everything from her hill,
- the **Vieux-Port** full of small colourful boats,
- **terracotta roofs** and pastel façades,
- **pine-covered hills** under a strong sun,
- the busy stalls of **Noailles**, the stadium, the islands on the horizon,
- **seagulls** everywhere, and the **Mistral** blowing things around.
Stylised, not realistic: simplified shapes, exaggerated colours, charming details.
 
## Colours and light
 
- **Mediterranean palette**: sea turquoise, deep navy, limestone white, sand gold, pine green, terracotta, sun yellow.
- **Warm light**: late-afternoon sun, soft long shadows, a slightly golden tint on everything.
- **Bright but never neon**: saturated, friendly colours; no harsh contrasts, no grey or gloomy tones, even in "problem" scenes (a flooded beach is still drawn under a clearing sky).
- **One colour per theme**: water feels blue, nature green, fire and forest warm orange, waste and markets earthy, so the player learns the world by colour.
## Characters
 
- **Cute and expressive**: chibi proportions, big heads, readable faces, lively poses.
- **Real people of Marseille**: diverse skin tones, hairstyles, ages and outfits, looking like people you'd actually meet in the city.
- **Each job has its signature prop**: binoculars, sample tube, hard hat, tablet, compost fork, diving mask. You understand the job before reading a word.
- **Mostly women in the jobs**, shown naturally and confidently, never as a message written on screen.
- Characters feel **friendly and approachable**, like a cool older cousin explaining their job, not a teacher.
## UI look
 
- UI elements feel **handmade and tactile**, like objects from the world: a leather notebook with lined paper, wooden or limestone signposts, postcard-style cards, stamps, a scroll for the quest.
- Pixel borders, rounded corners, soft drop shadows.
- Buttons look **pressable and inviting**, warm terracotta or sun-gold.
- Clean and uncluttered: the world stays visible, the UI never feels like a school form or a website.
## Motion and life
 
The world is **always alive**. Nothing is ever completely still:
 
- characters breathe, blink and bounce slightly,
- seagulls fly by, waves move, boats rock, clouds drift, leaves fall,
- the Mistral sometimes sweeps across the screen.
Movement is **soft and bouncy**, never stiff: things pop in with a little overshoot, glide rather than jump, and every click gets a small, satisfying reaction.
 
**Big moments are celebrated**: meeting someone new, finding the right job and finishing a quest come with stamps, sparkles and confetti. Everything else stays gentle, so the rewards feel special.
 
## Voice (for any text on screen)
 
- Short, simple, friendly sentences, like talking to a 13-year-old.
- A touch of local flavour and humour, without clichés.
- Always positive and encouraging, never preachy or guilt-inducing.
## Avoid
 
- Dark, grey, apocalyptic imagery or "sad planet" visuals
- Realistic or corporate look, stock-photo feeling, infographic style
- Brand logos (football club, companies) or art borrowed from existing games
- Pink-for-girls clichés or stereotyped characters
- Static, empty screens
